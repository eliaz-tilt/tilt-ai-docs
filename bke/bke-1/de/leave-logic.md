Delaware PFML : Job Protection and Income Benefits begin 1/1/26 Here’s what the
system needs to make a compliant eligibility decision:

1. Qualifying reasons Employee’s own serious health condition\
   Excludes employees injured at work, receiving WC benefits Family caregiving
   for parent, child, spouse (same as FMLA) Parental/bonding (birth, adoption,
   foster) within 1 year of birth/placement UPDATE 11/6/25: “Parental” is
   defined broadly for DE PFML purposes, so birthing parents can take parental
   leave as of the date of birth, instead of applying a medical (pregnancy
   disability) period first Qualifying military exigency (FMLA definition)

2. Employer Coverage (threshold determination) Coverage tiers (legal baseline –
   previous 12 months): 10–24 DE employees → Parental leave only (UPDATE 11/6:
   this includes what is typically the medical recovery period for birthing
   parents) However, employers in this tier can voluntarily provide coverage for
   the other three reasons ≥25 DE employees → All four qualifying reasons

Excluded employers <10 DE employees Federal government Businesses closed in
their entirety ≥30 consecutive days/year

Operational cadence\
Headcount measured first business day of each quarter. When crossing a
threshold: Employer must give 30 days’ notice to employees within 10 days of the
change. Tier Upgrades (crossing 10 or 25): coverage begins the first day of the
next quarter. Tier Downgrades: only after being below the threshold for four
consecutive quarters, then effective the first day of the next quarter.

3. Employee eligibility\
   Work state: DE Tenure: At least 12 months (need not be consecutive - same
   rule as FMLA) Hours: At least 1,250 hours worked for that employer in
   previous 12 months Some public sector employees excluded: employees in
   positions covered under Title 29 §5903(17)a (and Title 14 equivalents) are
   not “employees” for this program These are certain state government roles,
   and equivalent roles in public education where employees don’t pay PFML
   contributions or receive benefits ** System could populate a DE PFML plan in
   “pending” status at this point** When all docs are submitted, the state will
   advise the employer of their opinion; the employer has 5 business days to
   approve/deny in the state portal; an employee can appeal denials to the
   state.

4. Duration & frequency caps Leave Year Method = unsettled but expected to be
   measured forward

Maximum benefits in an application year = 12 weeks total

Parental: up to 12 weeks per application year

Non-parental: 6 weeks of each in any 24-month measured forward period, so:
Medical: up to 6 weeks in 24 months Caregiving: up to 6 weeks in 24 months
Qualifying exigency: up to 6 weeks in 24 months All subject to overall “12 weeks
per application year ceiling” Also, If both parents work for the same employer,
the employer may limit their combined parental, family caregiving, or QE leave
to 12 weeks in any 52-week period.

5. Wage replacement (benefit amount) Weekly benefit: 80% of average weekly wages
   for the last 4 completed calendar quarters immediately preceding the
   application Minimum: $100/week (or full wage if AWW < $100) Maximum:
   $900/week in 2026 & 2027 PTO: employer may not require PTO to be used first;
   top-off only by mutual agreement Wage base: contributions and benefits are
   based only on Delaware FICA wages\
   Online calculator: https://labor.delaware.gov/delaware-paid-leave/

6. Intermittent / reduced schedule Medical / caregiving: allowed only when
   medically necessary, supported by certification; benefits prorated based on
   workweek Parental: intermittent/reduced only if employer permits Minimum
   increment for intermittent leave: 1 full workday (but may be able to use FMLA
   for shorter absences) Note: reduced schedule leaves can be used in partial
   day increments

7. Coordination (FMLA, employer plans, STD/LTD) If leave also qualifies under
   FMLA, it runs concurrently (not stacked) Employer may require coordination
   with disability/family care plans or policies with written notice; total pay
   ≤100% AWW PFML is primary payor unless a policy explicitly states otherwise
   consistent with law

8. Application Process - Public Plan (Sec 12.5 of Regs)

This info is still in flux (a “claims adjudication” section will be added to
this guide by 1/1/26) but it seems settled that: 1) the state requires providers
to submit med certs to them electronically, and 2) employers have a say in the
approval or denial. Employee files in state portal. The online system
distributes the claim to the employee, employer, and Division. (1401-12.1–12.3)
Medical certification timing. If the health care provider hasn’t submitted a
completed certification within 20 days, the system notifies employee & employer
and the employee must follow up. If still not received 30 days after the claim
was submitted, the system marks it “Denied Due to Lack of Certification.” If the
certification arrives within 60 days after that denial flag, the claim is
automatically revived (no new claim needed). (1401-12.5.5) Employer adjudication
window. Once all required info is in (including the certification), the portal
issues an advisory; the employer has 5 business days to approve/deny based on
the totality of circumstances. (1401-4.3.1–4.3.3; 1401-12.5.6) Decision entry &
documentation. Employer records the decision in the portal; if approving, upload
supporting docs within 3 business days. (1401-4.3.4–4.3.7) Employee notice. The
portal/email provides the approval notice; if denying, employer must give the
reason and appeal info. (1401-4.3.5–4.3.8) First payment timing. The Division
makes the first payment within 30 days after the employer notifies the Division
of approval; then at least every 2 weeks. (1401-12.6)
